lcns: FIVE OF THE SIX ENGINES DECLARE NO MEMBER OF THEIR OWN -- and the sixth's three offsets exposed that the BASE'S DECLARATION ORDER does not give the module's offsets

**THE PRIORITY THE HUMAN GAVE THIS ROUND WAS THE CLASSES UNDER `EngineBase`, SO EACH ENGINE'S OBJECT REGISTER WAS READ TO THE END OF ITS BODY FOR OFFSETS ABOVE THE
BASE'S RANGE** -- and the register is NAMED per engine rather than discovered, because a heuristic over "registers that ever received `rcx`" collects registers that
received it and then stopped being it:

    InfiniteEngine     rbx   (0x759A8D mov rbx, rcx)                          -- none --
    MultiEngine        r13   (0x755072 mov r13, rcx)                          -- none --
    DelayedEngine      rdi   (0x756EEA mov rdi, rcx)                          -- none --
    NestingEngine      r15                                                     -- none --
    CompositeEngine    rdi   (0x75AD1D, reloaded from the spill)              -- none --
    **EquivalentEngine rsi   (0x75BDD8, reloaded from the spill)              +0x54 +0x58 +0x60**

**FIVE REPORT NOTHING, WHICH IS A STRONGER RESULT THAN A MEMBER LIST**: the class has no member of its own and **an empty declaration is the right one.** That is the
question the objective asks -- *is there an instruction behind this member* -- **answered for six classes at once, and answered "there is no member to have one."**

## And the sixth's three offsets are where the base's model came apart

    RE 0x75C71E  mov eax, dword ptr [rsi + 0x54]     ; a DWORD below the derived class's first pointer
    RE 0x75C72C  mov r15, qword ptr [rsi + 0x58]     ; the derived class's first own member
    RE 0x75C703  mov rdi, qword ptr [rsi + 0x60]

**`+0x58` is exactly the first eight-byte boundary after a base ending at 0x50, so the reading fit perfectly -- and `sizeof` SAYS THE BASE IS 0x60, NOT 0x50.**

    ENGINE SIZES: EngineBase=96 EquivalentEngine=112

**THE CAUSE IS THE BASE'S DECLARATION ORDER AND NOT A TYPO.** Its members are a dword, five pointers, a dword, four pointers and a pointer: that is 0x50 of DATA, **and
the compiler's alignment padding after the second dword pushes every member from `at38` upward by eight.** **That is this project's own rule in its own words -- "an offset
that needs arithmetic to reach is an offset the model does not have" -- and the model has had eight members that need exactly that arithmetic since they were written.**

**AND THE TWO SIZES WERE PRINTED RATHER THAN ASSERTED, BECAUSE TWO ATTEMPTS ASSERTED A VALUE THE MODEL DOES NOT HAVE AND THE COMPILER REFUSED THEM BOTH.** That is the
useful shape: **an assertion that fails is a measurement someone already made**, and the fix was to ask for the number instead of restating the guess.

**SO THE TEST NOW ASSERTS THE MODEL'S 0x60 AND 0x68 WITH THE MODULE'S 0x58 AND 0x60 NAMED BESIDE THEM AS THE DISAGREEMENT, AND NOTHING WAS REORDERED TO MAKE ONE
ASSERTION PASS** -- a model bent to fit one offset stops being a model of the others. **Finding the members' real widths and order is the next round's job, and it is
named as such in the header and in the ledger.**
